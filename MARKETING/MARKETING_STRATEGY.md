## PRIMARY AUDIENCE SEGMENTS

Ranked by expected conversion rate. Concentrate 70% of effort on Segments A–C.

### Segment A — "The Panicked Professional" *(Highest Priority)*
- **Profile:** 35–54, professional services (accountant, lawyer, financial planner, consultant), Sunshine Coast / SEQ, income $80k–$200k, 2–20 employees
- **Breach exposure:** ~62% of professional services credentials in major databases (LinkedIn 2016, Dropbox, Adobe, Canva AU)
- **Conversion trigger:** Fear of client data liability under OAIC NDB scheme. One breach notification = reputational destruction.
- **Expected CVR (warm):** 7–12%
- **Primary channel:** LinkedIn
- **[Source: ACSC 2022-23 Annual Report; OAIC NDB Q3 2025; Verizon DBIR 2024 | Confidence: High]**

### Segment B — "The Post-Optus/Medibank Anxiety Buyer"
- **Profile:** 25–54, any industry, personally affected by Optus (9.8M), Medibank (9.7M), or Latitude (14M) breaches
- **Market size:** ~11–13 million Australians directly affected by three mega-breaches
- **Expected CVR (targeted):** 9–16% — already breach-activated
- **Primary channel:** Meta (consumer), LinkedIn (business)
- **[Source: OAIC NDB Annual Report 2023; ACSC 2022-23 | Confidence: High]**

### Segment C — "The Healthcare Operator"
- **Profile:** 40–60, medical/dental/allied health practice owner, 1–15 staff, Sunshine Coast
- **Regulatory exposure:** Healthcare = consistently #1 or #2 in OAIC NDB notifications (~22% of all)
- **Expected CVR:** 11–18% (highest of all segments — regulatory urgency is the driver)
- **Primary channel:** LinkedIn, direct email to practice managers, local medical association networks
- **[Source: OAIC NDB Statistics Q1-Q4 2025; Health Records Act 2001 | Confidence: High]**

### Segment D — "The Tradie / Construction SME"
- **Profile:** 30–55, building/construction/trade services, 1–10 employees, income $60k–$150k
- **Challenge:** High scepticism of online purchases; referral-only effective pathway
- **Expected CVR (cold):** 2–4% | **(referral-introduced):** 12–18%
- **Primary channel:** Facebook community groups, BNI/Chamber of Commerce
- **[Source: ABS Business Characteristics Survey 2024; Master Builders QLD 2024 | Confidence: Medium]**

### Segment E — "The Retail/Hospitality Owner"
- **Profile:** 25–45, Sunshine Coast tourism/hospitality, thin margins, high staff turnover
- **Conversion trigger:** POS system exposure, staff credential reuse
- **Expected CVR:** 4–7%
- **Primary channel:** Facebook, local Chamber, direct email
- **[Source: ACSC 2022-23; Verizon DBIR 2024 | Confidence: Medium]**

## CHANNEL STRATEGY

### Priority Stack

```
PRIORITY 1: LinkedIn InMail (organic, personalised) — 57–70% open rate
PRIORITY 2: Cold email to SME list — volume play (30–42% open, breach-triggered subject)
PRIORITY 3: LinkedIn organic posts — brand/awareness layer
PRIORITY 4: Referral mechanics — lowest CAC, highest LTV
PRIORITY 5: LinkedIn paid — only justified as Surf-Check top-of-funnel
```

### LinkedIn Organic — $30 Breach Check funnel
- Organic post reach (1,000 connections): 400–800 impressions; with hashtags: 1,500–3,000
- Post conversion: 2,000 impressions × 0.7% CTR × 5% CVR = ~0.7 sales/post
- At 3 posts/week = ~2 Breach Check sales/week from organic LinkedIn alone

### LinkedIn InMail — SME outreach
- Open rate: 57–70% (vs 24–35% cold email)
- Tactical recommendation: 50 hyper-personalised InMails to Healthcare + Professional Services owners beats 500 cold emails for ROI
- **5-touch SME sequence (21-day cycle):**
  - Day 1: LinkedIn connection — peer-to-peer, no pitch
  - Day 3: LinkedIn DM — deliver a relevant threat stat for their sector
  - Day 7: Phone — "Do you have someone actively monitoring your external exposure?"
  - Day 10: Email — Breach Check one-pager, no pitch. "Thought this might be relevant."
  - Day 21: Final phone — "Last touchpoint from me — if timing is wrong, no issue."

### Cold Email — Volume play
- Personalised, breach-specific subject line: 30–42% open rate
- Open → Click: 6–10% (if references real breach relevant to their industry)
- Click → Purchase: 2–4%
- Revenue per 1,000 emails: ~$33 — marginally positive if list is free/organic
- **Uplift factors:** Subject line personalisation (+40–60% open rate), breach specificity (doubles click rate)

### Referral Program
- Referral conversion rate: 10–25% (highest of all channels)
- Referral LTV multiplier: 1.8× vs paid acquisition
- Viral coefficient target: 0.3–0.5 referrals per satisfied customer → self-sustaining at ~50 initial customers
- **Trigger email:** "You just found your stolen password. Know someone else at risk? Refer them."

### LinkedIn Paid — B2B only
- CPM AU cybersecurity: $35–$65 AUD | CPC: $8–$18 AUD
- At $30 product + 5% CVR: CAC = $160–$360 **— unprofitable for Breach Check alone**
- Justifiable only as Surf-Check ($249) upsell top-of-funnel

### Outreach Tone Standard
CyberSurf does not beg for time. CyberSurf shares intelligence and lets professionals decide. Every outreach must pass: *"Would a peer send this, or does it read like a door-to-door pitch?"*

## REFERRAL PARTNER PROGRAM

### Partner Categories
- Accountants and bookkeepers (BAS agents — see client financial exposure firsthand)
- Mortgage brokers (high-net-worth home segment)
- Financial planners (identity theft risk is a natural conversation)
- Solicitors/conveyancers (data handling compliance angle)
- Real estate property managers (rental owner segment)

### Referral Fee Structure

| Referral Type | Reward |
|---|---|
| Breach Check or Dark Web Monitoring | $30 credit or cash per activated client |
| Surf-Check Assessment | $100 flat fee on booking |
| Cyber Security Guard retainer | 10% of Month 1 retainer fee |

### Partner Opening Script
*"I'm not asking you to sell anything. I'm asking if you'd be comfortable telling a client who asks about cyber risk that there's a local expert they can call. If they become a client, there's a referral fee — but more importantly, you've protected your client relationship."*

### Compliance Flag
Before any referral partner agreement is signed: obtain legal advice on ASIC/AFS licensing obligations — referrals involving financial planners, mortgage brokers, or credit licensees may trigger Corporations Act or NCCP Act requirements. No verbal arrangements. All partners must execute written agreement with privacy consent clause. **This is a hard stop before signing any partner. See LEGAL/LEGAL_REGISTER.md.**

## BRAND STANDARDS (Summary)

- **Distinctive Assets (Byron Sharp):** Aqua #00d2ff is the primary brand asset — protect it. Sand white #fcfdf2 reads warmer than pure white — intentional, do not change without conversion evidence.
- **Tone:** Calm expert. Not alarming, not generic tech startup. Authoritative peer.
- **Never use:** competitor tool names in copy; scare tactics with no resolution path; tech jargon without plain-English follow-through
- **Forms:** Every field removes ~10% of completions. Justify each field before adding.
- **Copy benchmark:** Would a Sunshine Coast dentist with no IT background understand this in one read?

## COMPETITIVE POSITION

No Australian company currently offers a consumer-grade, one-time credential breach report with actual password visibility. CyberSurf is not entering a crowded market — it is creating a category. The primary sales challenge is client education: converting businesses who believe "our IT guy handles that" into clients who understand the distinction between IT operations and security risk management.

**White space confirmed:** No direct equivalent identified on the Sunshine Coast for a Fractional Security Officer service at this price point targeting households and SMEs. [Source: March 2026 competitive scan — revalidate at Phase 1 gate]
