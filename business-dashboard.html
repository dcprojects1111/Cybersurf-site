Australian Regulatory Context — Your Sales Advantage

Notifiable Data Breaches Scheme

Under the Privacy Act 1988, Australian businesses must notify the OAIC and affected individuals if a data breach is likely to cause serious harm. Small businesses with <$3M turnover are largely exempt — but their customers are still affected.

Sales angle: Many small businesses don't know if their staff or customer data has appeared in breach databases. CyberSurf fills this gap — especially for healthcare, legal, finance, and real estate sectors that handle sensitive data.

High-Value Target Sectors (Sunshine Coast)
  • Allied health / medical clinics — Privacy Act obligations
  • Real estate agencies — client financial data
  • Accountants & bookkeepers — ATO credential risk
  • Hospitality & tourism — high staff turnover, shared logins
  • Tradespeople with business email — personal brand risk
  • Retail & e-commerce — customer data handlers

These sectors have compliance reason to act — and budget to pay for it.

Priority Build Order

1. Auto Follow-up Email

After a report is run, auto-send a personalised email to the customer with their risk level and next steps. Triggers immediate trust and opens door to upsells.

~2–3 hrs to build. Add Resend API key to env vars.

2. Dark Web Monitoring (recurring)

Store baseline breach hashes per customer, run monthly re-check, email alert if new breach detected. Needs persistent DB (move from /tmp to PostgreSQL or SQLite on volume).

~1 day. Biggest recurring revenue lever.

3. Upsell Pages on Webapp

Add service offering cards to the report page — contextual upsells based on what was found. Password Manager Setup button appears if passwords were exposed. 2FA button for phone exposure.

~half a day. Pure revenue uplift.
