## SECTION 2 — THE PROBLEM: WHY THIS MARKET EXISTS NOW

### 2.1 The National Threat Context

- A cybercrime is reported in Australia **every six minutes** (ACSC Annual Cyber Threat Report, 2024–25).
- Queensland accounts for **28% of all Australian cybercrime reports** — disproportionately high relative to population share of 20%.
- The average cost of a successful cyberattack on a small business is now **$56,600** (ACSC, 2025), including recovery, lost productivity, and reputational damage.
- **95% of breaches are caused by human error** — phishing, weak credentials, and unpatched systems. This is not a technology failure. It is a training and process failure.

### 2.2 The Local Vulnerability Gap

Sunshine Coast SMEs and professional households share a structural vulnerability: they generate enough digital complexity to be attractive targets, but not enough revenue to justify a dedicated security professional.

- A qualified CISO or Security Officer costs **$130,000–$170,000 per year** in salary alone.
- A Sunshine Coast medical practice, law firm, or construction company with 8 staff cannot absorb that cost — and won't until after a breach forces the issue.
- **The $30 Tripwire exists because the gap between "no security" and "$150k/year hire" is where businesses get destroyed.**

### 2.3 The AI Escalation Factor

AI-generated phishing emails are now indistinguishable from legitimate correspondence. Scams that previously required a human operator to draft convincing copy now execute at machine scale. The 2025 Qantas and REST Super breach cycles demonstrated that even large, well-resourced organisations are not immune — and the personal data harvested from those breaches is now actively being weaponised against individuals and small businesses on the Sunshine Coast.

The **$30 Tripwire** is a monthly subscription delivering the minimum viable security layer that transforms a household or micro-business from "invisible target" to "hardened baseline."
